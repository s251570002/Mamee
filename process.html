03 / PROCESS

How a problem becomes
an innovation opportunity.

01

Identify the problem

Recognise the weak market response, changing consumer needs or operational pressure.

02

Root cause analysis

Ask why the problem exists instead of treating only its visible symptom.

03

Research + benchmarking

Study consumers, markets, competitors, technologies and partnership opportunities.

04

Ideation

Generate alternative ways to reposition products, create experiences or improve operations.

05

Testing + validation

Use consumer feedback, limited launches, market response and product development evidence.

06

Implementation

Scale the selected idea through production, partnerships, technology and market expansion.

CASE WALKTHROUGH

MAMEE Monster: observation → opportunity → product.

This is the clearest documented example of creativity in the case. MAMEE's official history says Tan Sri Pang Tee Chew noticed rubber tappers eating uncooked instant noodles from the pack. The company then changed direction and launched the crunchy MAMEE snack in 1974.

01

Identify

The original Lucky noodle business was struggling, while an unusual consumption behaviour was observed.

02

Root cause / insight

The product could be consumed without cooking and could potentially serve a different need: convenient snacking.

03

Research

Later MAMEE sources describe market research and a Monster Lab that works with consumer focus groups.

04

Ideation

The noodles were seasoned and repositioned as a crunchy snack rather than a conventional meal product.

05

Validation

The product's market response demonstrated that the new proposition resonated with consumers.

06

Implementation

MAMEE stopped Lucky production after Mamee Monster became a hit and continued developing the snack portfolio.

Research is not only about surveys.

MAMEE's documented innovation practices include direct observation of behaviour, consumer focus groups through Monster Lab, collaborations, limited product launches and attention to changing consumer habits.
